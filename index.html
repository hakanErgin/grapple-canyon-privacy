Grapple Canyon privacy policy

Last updated: September 15, 2026

Scope

This policy describes the Android player build being prepared for the next Grapple Canyon release, identified by package com.grapplecanyon.game, and the hosted web build at https://play.grapple-canyon.workers.dev. It is written for the code currently prepared as a Community-enabled release candidate, which includes the Community Levels service described below. If the app later adds an account, advertising, analytics, purchases, or another data flow, this policy must be reviewed and updated before that build is distributed.

Data kept locally by the app

The game keeps a small amount of player state in local device storage so the same installation can remember the player's choices and progress:

  • Sound preference: on or off (gc.sound.v1).
  • Theme preference: Auto, Day, or Night (gc.theme.v1).
  • Catalogue progression and unlocked tracks (gc.progress.v2).
  • A best time for each track (gc.best.v2.<track-id>).
  • The recent run ghost samples for each track (gc.ghost.v2.<track-id>).
  • The best-run ghost samples used for the best-time comparison (gc.ghostbest.v2.<track-id>).
  • The Full Run best time (gc.fullrun.v2).
  • Editor drafts and the custom levels you saved (gc.editor.draft.v1, gc.custom.v1).
  • Community Levels state: a random device identifier created the first time the game talks to the service (gc.device.v1); a cached copy of the last Community level lists you saw (gc.community.cache.v1); the votes you gave (gc.community.votes.v1); the levels and creators you chose to hide on this device (gc.community.blocks.v1); the levels you published together with the removal token for each (gc.published.v1); and the author name you last typed (gc.author.v1); and a local receipt recording acceptance of the current Community rules (gc.community.rules.accepted.v1, currently v1).

Ghost records are gameplay samples used to draw a replay on the same device; they are not a player profile or an online account. The current player catalogue contains five tracks. The game also keeps an in-memory copy while a session is running; if browser storage is unavailable, a new record is not promised to survive reload.

Community Levels service

The game includes a small level-sharing service so that players can publish levels they made in the Editor and play, vote on, and report levels made by other players. The service is a Cloudflare Worker reached at https://grapple-levels.grapple-canyon.workers.dev over HTTPS only. Cloudflare runs the Worker on its global network at the location nearest to the player; the service's database is a Cloudflare D1 database located in Cloudflare's Eastern Europe region. The service does not enable request logging.

There is no account. Requests carry the random device identifier above, which is not linked to a person, an email address, or any platform account. The service also derives a salted cryptographic hash (SHA-256 with a server-side secret) of the request's IP address and keeps only that hash; the raw IP address is not written to the database and the service keeps no request logs. Cloudflare, as the hosting and network provider, processes the IP address to deliver the request under its own privacy terms and acts as a processor for the project.

What the service stores, and when:

  • When you publish a level: the level data, the level name, the optional author name you typed, the device identifier, the IP hash, the time, and a hash of the removal token that only your device holds. Level names and author names are limited to a short set of plain characters.
  • When you vote: the level, the device identifier, the vote direction, and the time. One vote per level per device; clearing a vote removes the record.
  • When you report a level: the level, the IP hash, the device identifier, and the time. One report per level per IP hash.
  • When you report a creator: the source level, the private publisher device identifier resolved by the service from that level, the reporter's IP hash, the reporter's device identifier, and the time. The publisher device identifier and report record are private; they are not returned to players. One report per creator target and IP hash, even when the creator has published more than one level.
  • When you play a community level: a play count on the level is increased. No per-player play record is stored.
  • Rate limiting: per-minute request counters keyed by IP hash. They are only used for about two minutes and an hourly cleanup deletes the expired ones, so a counter exists for at most about an hour.

Published levels, their names, author names, vote totals, and play counts are public to every player of the game. Do not put personal information in a level name or author name. Level data is public content once published: other players can play it, and the game may keep a cached copy of the lists on their devices. Each public level response also includes a stable, opaque creator identifier. The service derives that identifier by hashing the publisher's random app/device identifier; the app uses it to group levels for its local creator block and report controls. The raw publisher device identifier is never exposed to players.

The service hides a level once enough players report it or vote it down, or when nobody plays it for about a month. Hidden levels are deleted after ninety days. A level you remove with Remove online is hidden at once and deleted after one day. Level votes and level reports are deleted together with their level. Creator reports are retained for up to ninety days and are cleaned up independently of the source level, so removing a source level does not remove the private creator-report incident before its own retention limit. Vote records are also deleted when the voter clears the vote.

What the current app does not collect

The current player build has no account or login, advertising, analytics or telemetry, remote logging, purchases, or remote runtime asset service. Apart from the Community Levels requests above, it does not send the local state listed earlier to a Grapple Canyon server; progress, best times, and ghost records stay on the device. The Android package requests the INTERNET permission for the Community Levels service and no other permission, and the current project disables cleartext traffic.

These statements describe the current build and are not a promise about a future version with different functionality.

Feedback email

If a user chooses to contact the project at hakan.ergin@gmail.com, the message and any information the user includes are handled by the user's and the email provider's services under their own terms. The game does not automatically collect or transmit feedback, and a user can choose what to include in an email.

Retention, reset, and deletion

Local state remains on the device until the user changes it, uses the game's reset control, clears the app's data, or uninstalls the app, subject to the device operating system's behavior.

In the player build, Reset progress clears catalogue progression and unlocks, per-track best times, recent and best ghost records, and the Full Run best. It preserves the sound and theme preferences and the Community Levels state listed above (device identifier, cached lists, votes, published levels and their removal tokens, author name, local Community blocks, Community rules acceptance receipt, Editor drafts, and custom levels). Clearing app data or uninstalling removes the app's local state under the current Android no-backup contract, including the removal tokens; a level whose token is lost can no longer be removed from that device. There is no Grapple Canyon account or server-side profile to delete.

To remove a level you published, use Remove online in the level list's Custom section on the device that published it. To ask for the removal of a level or record you cannot remove yourself, contact the address in the Contact section and name the level and, if you know it, the author name. The owner can hide the level from the service, after which the ninety-day deletion applies, and can delete a level and its votes and reports outright on request.

Security and offline posture

The app uses the platform's local storage for the state above. Its only network service is Community Levels, reached over HTTPS; the app works offline apart from that section. No storage method can guarantee absolute security; users should keep their device and operating system protected. The Android project records android:allowBackup="false" and android:usesCleartextTraffic="false"; these are implementation facts to recheck for the exact release candidate, not a promise about every device backup mechanism.

Changes to this policy
